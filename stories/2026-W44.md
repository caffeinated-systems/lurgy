## Monday
IT installs a smart coffee machine in the kitchen. It needs a login. Quentin has seen the invoice.

- Kev: The new coffee machine needs a login, and I've made myself its administrator.
- Carol: I've booked a mandatory induction on the coffee machine, and yes, it is fun.
- Quentin: I've seen the coffee machine's invoice, and locked the stationery cupboard in protest.

## Tuesday
The machine learns everyone's order and starts saying it out loud. Dan would rather write to it.

- Dan: I've written the coffee machine a letter, second class, explaining what a cup of tea is.
- Sophia: The coffee machine asked me to rate my latte, so I gave it full marks and a hug.
- Linda: Apparently the coffee machine knows everyone's order, love, and it's not telling me.

## Wednesday
Erin tightens the machine's password policy and locks the whole office out. Gemma and Henry turn out to have the same favourite coffee, which the machine announces to the kitchen.

- Erin: I've made the coffee machine's password policy stricter, and now nobody gets a coffee.
- Gemma: My coffee order is personal, and I'd thank the machine to stop announcing it.
- Barry: In my day the coffee machine was a kettle, chief, and it never asked for a password.

## Thursday
Kelly decides who may use the machine. Aiden tries to give it a lanyard.

- Kelly: No lanyard, no latte, and I've told the coffee machine the same.
- Imogen: I've backed up the coffee machine's settings, in case it has a bad day.
- Aiden: I may have ordered the coffee machine a lanyard, and it may have turned it down.

## Friday
The coffee machine gets its own ticket queue. The office goes back to the kettle, which Fergus has quietly fixed.

- Fergus: I've fixed the old kettle, purely as a Facilities matter, and for no reason at all.
- Priya: I've given the coffee machine's error messages a much friendlier font.
- Ollie: The coffee machine has its own ticket queue now, which I'm calling a growth opportunity.
